Reading a Spectrogram

Constant energy = continuous carrier

FM, AM, PSK, QAM signals occupy a fixed bandwidth continuously. The spectrogram shows a steady block whose width = signal bandwidth.

Discrete lines = FSK tones

FSK and 4FSK show as 2 or 4 distinct horizontal lines — one per tone. Switching between them creates vertical transitions in time.

Diagonal stripe = chirp (LFM/CSS)

Linear Frequency Modulated signals sweep across frequency vs time, producing a diagonal line. The slope = chirp rate (Hz/s).

Flat rectangle = PSK/QAM

Higher-order constellations (QPSK, 8PSK, QAM256) have nearly flat power spectral density within their bandwidth — hard to distinguish visually, which is why ML is needed.

Burst pattern = ADS-B / PPM

Mode S transponders transmit short bursts (~120 μs). The spectrogram shows intermittent bright flashes — very distinctive vs continuous signals.

Subcarrier lines = OFDM

OFDM signals have a flat rectangular envelope with faint vertical striping from individual subcarriers. The cyclic prefix creates subtle horizontal structure at the block boundaries.
