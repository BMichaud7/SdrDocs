Overall Accuracy vs SNR

Mean per-class accuracy across all 47 signal classes at each SNR level. The model reaches 77% accuracy at SNR = 10 dB and saturates near 85% above 15 dB. Below 0 dB the problem becomes very hard — constellation structure collapses into noise.

Per-Class Accuracy (all SNRs mixed)

Accuracy for each of the 47 signal classes, averaged over all SNR levels from −10 to +30 dB. ADS-B and DTMF score highest due to their distinctive spectral signatures. QAM64, PSK31, P25 Phase 2 and NAVTEX are the hardest — primarily due to overlap with neighbouring classes at low SNR.

Hard Classes Analysis

Classes with accuracy below 20% need targeted improvement in v2 training. The common root causes are constellation overlap (QAM family), insufficient training examples (new protocol classes), and ambiguous spectral shape at the symbol rates used.

Classes with accuracy < 50%

Sorted by accuracy ascending — these benefit most from v2 improvements (1024-sample input, 4× QAM boost, 2× dataset)

QAM64 — 6.6%Worst class. Constellation is between QAM32 and QAM256 — the model confuses it with both at low SNR. V2 training adds 4× oversampling and 1024-sample context (sees more full symbols).
PSK31 — 0.0%31.25 baud — at 512 samples only ~16 symbols captured (not enough for Varicode pattern). 1024 samples doubles to ~32 symbols → should improve significantly.
P25_PHASE2 — 0.0%π/4-DQPSK — confused with QPSK at low SNR. Likely needs symbol-rate-aware features or more training examples in the new protocol-class data.
NAVTEX — 12.2%100 baud FSK — very slow, only a few symbol transitions visible in 512 samples. 1024 samples should help significantly.
RTTY — 13.7%45.45 baud — similar issue to NAVTEX (very slow baud rate). More samples per window = clearer tone switching pattern.
16PSK — 11.0%16-point PSK constellation is very close to QAM16 in feature space. A deeper/wider model (256 channels) should better resolve the boundary.
